Problem Statement
The Challenge: Why Black-Box AI Fails Indian Agriculture

1. Lack of Explainability

  • Generic ML classification models offer black-box predictions without agronomic justification.
  • Farmers and agricultural extension officers cannot verify why one crop was recommended over another.
  • Lack of transparency breeds distrust and low adoption rates among farming communities.

2. The Missing Data Trap

  • Over 70% of smallholder farmers lack recent laboratory N-P-K soil test records.
  • Standard ML algorithms coerce null values to zero (N=0, P=0, K=0), fatally distorting recommendations.
  • Farmers are unfairly penalized for missing data, leading to severe crop failure risks.

3. Agro-Climatic Heterogeneity

  • India spans 15 broad agro-climatic zones with stark regional variances.
  • Monsoon Kharif vs cool Rabi vs hot Zaid cycles dictate physiological success.
  • Algorithms that ignore physiological temperature bounds or soil pH thresholds lead to ruined harvests.

Our Solution
Smart Krishi Advisor: Transparent & Trustworthy Decision Support

🌱 Grounded in Agronomy

  • Built upon verified agronomic parameters published by ICAR and TNAU.

Engine Methodology
Multi-Factor Agronomic Weighting & Scoring Model

Factor Allocation Matrix (100% Total Weight)

  • Growing Season (20%): Kharif, Rabi, or Zaid physiological cycle alignment.
  • Soil Texture & Type (15%): Clay Loam, Black Vertisol, Alluvial, Sandy Loam suitability.
  • Soil pH Range (15%): Optimal vs tolerable acidity/alkalinity tolerance curves.
  • Temperature Suitability (15%): Crop vegetative and reproductive thermal limits.
  • Rainfall & Moisture (15%): Cumulative seasonal rainfall vs water demand.
  • Relative Humidity (10%): Atmospheric moisture and disease predisposition.
  • NPK Nutrients (10%): Nitrogen (3.33%), Phosphorus (3.33%), Potassium (3.34%).

Mathematical Rigor & Normalization

  • Formula: Score = (Evaluated Points / Evaluated Weights) × 100
  • Example: Farmer without NPK Soil Kit:
  • Evaluated weight denominator dynamically adjusts to 90 (100 - 10).
  • Data coverage is surfaced as 90% without artificial penalty.
  • Suitability Tiers:
  • • Highly Compatible: Score ≥ 80%
  • • Moderately Compatible: Score 55% – 79%
  • • Potential Mismatch: Score < 55%

Robustness & Safety
Safety First: Handling Incomplete Data & Input Guards

Transparent Data Coverage

  • Tracks the exact percentage of agronomic parameters provided by the farmer.
  • Distinguishes measured laboratory data from observational estimates.
  • Informs farmers of confidence levels before capital investment in seeds and fertilizers.

Insufficient Data Barrier

  • Halts recommendations if data coverage drops below the 35% confidence threshold.
  • Eliminates reckless hallucinated predictions when crucial data is omitted.
  • Generates a prioritized checklist of missing variables needed (e.g. soil pH, rainfall).

Strict Schema Validation

  • Pydantic schemas enforce strict biological and physical constraints:
  • • Soil pH bounded strictly between 0.0 and 14.0.
  • • Relative humidity constrained between 0.0% and 100.0%.
  • • Immediate user-friendly guidance on invalid boundary entries.
